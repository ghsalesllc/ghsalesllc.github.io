Japanese Sugi vs. Western Red Cedar for Saunas and Decks

How the two most popular sauna and decking softwoods compare, and how to choose between them.

Call 208-606-0899Text us
Buyer's guide

Two "cedars" that aren't true cedars

If you're choosing wood for a sauna or a deck, you'll often see Japanese Sugi and western red cedar side by side. They have a lot in common: both are light, soft, easy-to-work softwoods in the cypress family, and neither is a true cedar. Both work well for saunas and outdoor projects. The differences come down to look, scent, availability and price. Here's an honest comparison.

For saunas

Both woods are popular sauna choices because they're light and low in density, so they don't feel as hot to the touch as dense woods. Things to weigh:

  • Scent: western red cedar has a strong aroma that some people love and others find too much in a hot room. Sugi's scent is milder.
  • Look: Sugi's sapwood and heartwood stripes give a lively, natural look. Western red cedar can look warmer and more uniform in clear grades, but lower grades can vary a lot.
  • Profile: our 1x6 clear grade Sugi tongue and groove ($1.95/lin ft, 95%+ clear, occasional tight knots may appear) is reversible, with a V-groove on one face and a nickel-gap look on the other.

Whichever you pick, many builders leave sauna interiors unfinished or use a sauna-specific finish, and keep film finishes off benches. Estimate your boards with the sauna wood calculator.

For decks and outdoor projects

Both are used for decks, fences, planters and siding. Neither is as hard as a hardwood or composite, so expect some dents and wear. Things that help either one last outdoors:

  • Keep good airflow under the deck and leave gaps between boards so they dry out.
  • Use stainless or coated fasteners; plain steel can react with cedar-type woods and leave dark stains.
  • Apply an exterior finish if you want to keep the color; otherwise both weather to gray.

We carry Sugi decking in 5/4x6 (a true 1 in. thick, $2/lin ft) and 2x6 ($2.65/lin ft). See our Sugi decking page, or browse redwood, cedar and composite options in our decking section.

Like any wood, wear a dust mask and eye protection when cutting or sanding.
