What Is SPC Rigid Core Flooring? A Treasure Valley Buyer's Guide

Call 208-606-0899Text us

Quick answer: SPC (stone-plastic composite) rigid core flooring is a type of luxury vinyl plank (LVP) built around a dense, stiff core made mostly of limestone powder and PVC. It's waterproof, it clicks together as a floating floor, and its stiff core hides small subfloor bumps better than flexible vinyl. When you compare SPC planks, look at the wear layer (in mil) first, then total thickness (in mm), then the pad and warranty. "Rigid core," "SPC," "WPC," "LVP": flooring labels pile up fast. Here's what each one means, how to read the specs on the box, and how to pick the right plank for your rooms.

What the labels mean

  • LVP (luxury vinyl plank): the whole family of wood-look vinyl planks. Some are thin and flexible (glue-down); some have a rigid core.
  • Rigid core: an LVP with a stiff core layer, so it can float over the subfloor as a click-together floor. SPC and WPC are both rigid core.
  • SPC (stone-plastic composite): the core is mostly limestone powder bonded with PVC. It's dense and hard, resists dents from furniture, and stays stable through temperature changes.
  • WPC (wood-plastic composite): the core is PVC blended with foaming agents (despite the name, usually no real wood). It's lighter and a bit softer and warmer underfoot, but dents more easily than SPC.
  • Wear layer: the clear top coat that protects the printed wood look, measured in mil (thousandths of an inch). This is the number that decides how well the floor stands up to wear.
  • Attached pad: a factory underlayment on the back of the plank. It adds comfort and quiet, and usually means you don't need separate underlayment (check the product's instructions).

How an SPC plank is built (top to bottom)

  1. Wear layer: clear protective coat (12 mil, 20 mil, etc.)
  2. Print layer: the wood-look image
  3. SPC core: stone-plastic composite, gives the plank its stiffness
  4. Attached pad: on many planks, for sound and comfort

Reading thickness and wear layer

The wear layer matters more than total thickness. A 6 mm plank with a 12 mil wear layer and a 5 mm plank with a 20 mil wear layer are built for different jobs: the thicker wear layer resists scratches and scuffs better, while the thicker core feels a bit more solid underfoot. For a refresher on reading mm and mil, see LVP vs. waterproof laminate.

A simple rule of thumb:

  • 12 mil: bedrooms, guest rooms, offices, lighter-traffic rooms and many rentals
  • 20 mil and up: kitchens, entries, hallways, homes with kids and dogs, and anywhere you want the floor to look new longer

6 steps to choose an SPC floor

1. List your rooms and how hard they're used

Write down each room, whether it gets water (kitchen, bath, laundry, entry, basement), and how much traffic, pets and kids it sees. Busy, wet rooms point to a thicker wear layer.

2. Pick a wear layer first

Choose 20 mil or more for busy rooms and 12 mil where traffic is light. If you want one floor through the whole house, size it for your busiest room.

3. Check the subfloor

SPC hides small bumps, but it still needs a flat, dry, sound subfloor. Over concrete slabs or crawlspaces, follow the product's moisture and underlayment instructions.

4. Decide on plank size

Standard 7"x48" fits most rooms. 9"x60" XL planks show more of the wood look per board and suit larger, open rooms.

5. Choose in stock or special order

In-stock rigid-core LVP is ready to pick up and priced lowest when a lot fits your project; quantities are limited, so check each listing for available square footage. Special order (like MSI Prescott or Cyrus) gives you a wide choice of colors, ordered in the amount your project needs, with matching transitions, stair nosing and trim, still priced well below typical flooring stores.

6. See samples and plan the install

Look at samples in your own light, bring room measurements, and add extra for cuts. Then decide whether you'll install it yourself or have it installed.

SPC flooring checklist

  • Rooms listed with water exposure and traffic level
  • Wear layer chosen (12 mil for light use, 20 mil+ for busy rooms)
  • Total thickness and attached pad compared
  • Subfloor checked: flat, dry and sound
  • Plank size picked (7"x48" or 9"x60" XL)
  • In stock vs special order decided
  • Square footage measured, plus extra for cuts
  • Transitions, stair nosing and trim added for special-order floors
  • Samples seen in person
  • Install plan decided: DIY or installation

Frequently asked questions

What does SPC stand for in flooring?

SPC stands for stone-plastic composite. It's the rigid core inside many luxury vinyl planks, made mostly of limestone powder and PVC, which makes the plank stiff, dense and waterproof.

Is SPC better than WPC?

SPC is denser and resists dents better, so it's a strong choice for kitchens, rentals and busy homes. WPC is a bit softer and warmer underfoot. Both are waterproof rigid-core planks; the wear layer matters more than the core for scratch resistance.

Is SPC rigid core flooring waterproof?

Yes, SPC planks are waterproof. Water can still get under any floating floor at the edges or from a leak, so follow the installation instructions for perimeter gaps and moisture protection.

What thickness of LVP should I buy?

Look at the wear layer before total thickness. A 20 mil wear layer suits kitchens, entries and homes with pets; 12 mil works for lighter-traffic rooms. For example, MSI Prescott is 6.5 mm with a 20 mil wear layer, and MSI Cyrus is 5 mm with a 12 mil wear layer.
